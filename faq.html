Working together

What does an AI Solutions Engineer do?

An AI Solutions Engineer designs, builds and runs AI systems that do real work inside a business: agents, voice and chat assistants, automations and reporting pipelines. The job covers the whole path from a business problem to a tested system in production, not only the model or the prompt.

In my practice that means writing the specification, building in a test environment, verifying the result, and launching with monitoring and a known cost per run.

What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

A Forward Deployed Engineer works inside the client's business rather than from the outside. Instead of handing over recommendations or a product and leaving, I embed with your team, work in your actual systems and data, build the solution in your environment, and iterate with the people who will run it. Deliverables are working systems, not slide decks.

It's the way I work with my agency clients today: building inside their CRM, reviewing results with the account managers each day, and handing off documentation and tests so the team owns what was built.

Who do you work with?

Mostly marketing agencies, in-house marketing and revenue-operations teams, and professional-services firms. Agencies are a natural fit because I've built AI systems that run across an entire client portfolio.

How long does a build take?

An AI Readiness Sprint takes two weeks. Most single-system builds take two to six weeks from approved spec to handoff. Some move faster: a nightly account-health dashboard went live in about a week, and a 20+ page website launched in 30 days.

How is pricing structured?

Sprints and builds are fixed-scope and fixed-price after discovery. Ongoing work is a monthly retainer. For systems with running costs, such as voice agents, launch, build and maintenance are priced separately and AI usage is billed at cost. I share a quote after our first call.

Do you work with businesses outside Madison?

Yes. I'm based in Madison, Wisconsin and work with clients across the U.S. by video. Madison-area clients can meet in person for kickoffs.

What's the first step?

Book a 30-minute call. Bring the process you'd like AI to handle. We'll talk through the data, the risks and what success is worth, and you'll leave knowing whether it's worth building.

AI systems & safety

How do you make sure an AI agent won't do something wrong?

Several layers:

  • Agents that change things are built and tested in separate test environments first, with checks that confirm where they're working.
  • Reporting systems are read-only by default.
  • Client-facing agents pass a written test suite (50+ cases for a voice agent) and a safety regression before anyone outside the team hears them.
  • When an input looks wrong, the system stops and notifies a person instead of guessing.
What does it cost to run an AI system after launch?

It depends on volume, and I measure it rather than estimate it. Every pipeline I build tracks its own model and data costs per run. For example, a full AI research pull on a new client costs about $4–8. A 33-agent platform serving an entire agency runs for about $32–42 a month.

How do you handle data privacy and security?
  • API keys stay on the server, never in the browser.
  • Agents get the minimum access they need, often read-only.
  • Sensitive items such as tax IDs and passwords never go through forms or AI prompts.
  • AI-researched facts are labeled verified, derived or needs input, and unverified details are never presented as fact.
Do you build voice AI agents?

Yes. I design and test inbound phone agents that answer questions, book appointments, take messages and transfer to a person with a spoken summary. They capture consent before follow-up texts and have clear rules about what they won't say, such as prices or technical advice.

Can AI agents build inside a CRM like GoHighLevel?

Yes, with the right method. I write a specification for every object, and Claude or OpenAI Codex builds it directly in the platform. After each phase an API readback and gap report show what was built and what wasn't. One unattended run lasted 3.5 hours, and the two-day build produced 351 custom values, a 12-stage pipeline and a 52-entry knowledge base.

Should we build or buy?

Buy the commodity and own the interpretation. If a tool already measures something well, use it. Build where your data, process or judgment is the advantage. An AI Readiness Sprint ends with a clear build-or-buy call for each opportunity.

What is MCP, and why does it matter?

The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard that lets AI assistants such as Claude securely use your data and tools. I've built an MCP server in production use that gives Claude access to an agency's client profiles, with token authentication and audit logging.
